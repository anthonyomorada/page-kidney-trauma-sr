Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, entitled “Traumatic Page Kidney – A Systematic Review from a Level I Trauma Center,” for consideration in Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery. This article presents a comprehensive systematic review of traumatic Page kidney, including cases due to iatrogenic causes, and provides an in-depth analysis of factors influencing management and outcomes. To our knowledge, this is the first review to aggregate and critically examine all reported trauma-related Page kidney cases in the literature, an effort which we believe will be of great interest to the trauma and acute care community.

Significance and Relevance: Page kidney is a rare phenomenon where an external compression of the kidney (often by a hematoma) leads to hypertension. While over a hundred cases have been documented since 1939, traumatic causes (such as blunt renal injuries or post-procedural hematomas) represent a substantial subset that trauma surgeons may encounter. Because initial trauma management often prioritizes organ preservation, the delayed hypertensive complication can be easily missed. Our review synthesizes decades of global experience to highlight predictors of when conservative management is safe versus when timely surgical intervention is necessary. We also detail time-to-diagnosis patterns, emphasizing the need for long-term blood pressure follow-up in patients with renal injuries – a recommendation directly relevant to trauma practice guidelines.

Key Findings: Our systematic search yielded approximately 30 trauma-related Page kidney cases. We found that early recognition and appropriate intervention can preserve renal function in most instances, whereas delayed diagnosis often results in nephrectomy or permanent hypertension. We identified several key predictors of management outcomes (e.g., injury severity, single-kidney status, degree of hypertension) and propose a practical framework to guide clinicians in similar cases. Additionally, we bring attention to pediatric trauma-induced hypertension and the importance of post-trauma monitoring. We believe these insights fill an important gap in the trauma literature, given that existing discussions of renal trauma rarely address the hypertensive complications after the acute phase.

Why JTACS: The readership of JTACS includes trauma surgeons, emergency physicians, and critical care specialists who are precisely the audience that would benefit from awareness of this condition. By publishing in JTACS, we aim to disseminate crucial knowledge that can improve trauma patient outcomes – specifically, to prevent missed diagnoses of Page kidney and to guide effective management when it occurs. Our manuscript combines clinical relevance with a thorough review methodology, aligning well with the journal’s mission to advance trauma care.
