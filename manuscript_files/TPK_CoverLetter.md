Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Traumatic Page Kidney: A Systematic Review and First Report of Bilateral Surgical Decompression" for consideration in the Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery. This work presents a comprehensive systematic review of 24 cases of trauma-induced Page kidney and reports the first documented bilateral case managed with surgical decompression. Page kidney, characterized by hypertension from renal compression by hematoma, represents a rare but treatable cause of post-traumatic complications that trauma surgeons may encounter but easily miss during standard follow-up protocols.

Our systematic review reveals that 87.5% of patients present with hypertension at a median of 15 days post-trauma, yet diagnosis is frequently delayed up to 9 years after the initial injury. We found that surgical decompression achieved the highest success rates among treatment modalities, with zero mortality reported across all cases. These findings provide practical guidance for trauma surgeons regarding when to suspect Page kidney and how to manage it effectively. Our bilateral case demonstrates that even the most complex presentations can be successfully treated with timely surgical intervention, preventing permanent renal damage and dialysis dependence.

This manuscript addresses a significant knowledge gap in trauma literature, where existing discussions of renal trauma rarely address delayed hypertensive complications. We believe the JTACS readership of trauma surgeons, emergency physicians, and critical care specialists represents the ideal audience for this work, as early recognition of this condition directly impacts patient outcomes. All authors have approved this submission, declare no conflicts of interest, and confirm this work has not been published previously nor is under consideration elsewhere.
